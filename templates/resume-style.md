# Resume Style Guide

Voice and formatting rules for all tailored resumes. The cover letter has `cover-letter-style.md`; this is the resume equivalent.

## Summary Voice

Your resume summary should read like a person talking about what they do and why they care, not a corporate bio.

### Rules

- Start with "I am" or "I" — never "Staff Software Engineer with 7+ years..." (third-person resume-speak)
- Talk about what you care about and love doing, not just accomplishments
- Show personality — a specific, honest reason you do the work is the right register
- Weave in teaching/mentoring naturally if it's part of your identity
- Embed metrics in narrative, don't list them: e.g., "a 300+ engineer monorepo depends on systems I own" not "managed systems for 300+ engineers"
- Mirror the posting's priorities in your own voice, not the posting's language verbatim
- Be precise about tenure at each company — don't conflate total career length with a single role's length

### Banned Phrases

These are corporate filler. Never use them in any resume prose (summary, bullets, skills descriptions):

- "track record of"
- "proven ability to"
- "demonstrated experience in"
- "leveraging" / "leverage"
- "synergies"
- "passionate about" (show the passion through specifics instead)
- "also builds" (when bridging to personal projects — integrate naturally)
- "results-driven"
- "self-motivated"
- "detail-oriented"

### Good Examples

> I am a Staff Software Engineer who genuinely cares about how other engineers experience their tools. Over my career I've gone from building product features to owning the developer platforms a 300+ engineer monorepo depends on...

> I'm the kind of engineer who notices that deploys take 45 minutes and can't stop until they take 15...

### Bad Examples

> Staff Software Engineer with 7+ years of experience building scalable platforms. Proven track record of leading cross-functional migrations and driving developer productivity improvements.

> Results-driven engineer with demonstrated experience in observability, CI/CD, and identity systems.

## Punctuation

- Go easy on em-dashes. One per resume is fine; more than that and it becomes a crutch. Use periods, colons, or commas instead. If you catch yourself reaching for an em-dash, try a period first.

## Bullet Style

- Lead with the action and outcome, not the technology
- Be specific: name the system, the scope, the number
- One bullet = one accomplishment. Don't chain unrelated wins with semicolons
- Prefer active verbs: "Designed," "Built," "Led," "Reduced," "Drove" — not "Responsible for," "Involved in," "Helped with"
- Quantify where possible but never fabricate — check any "Note on Unverified Metrics" section in master-resume.md before using dollar figures or percentages

## Skills Section

- Only list skills relevant to the specific posting
- Group logically: Languages, Frontend, APIs & Data, Infrastructure, AI Tooling
- Drop categories that don't apply (e.g., skip "AI Tooling" if the posting doesn't mention it)
- Include skills from personal projects when they fill a gap the posting asks for

## Formatting & Length

- One page, 10pt Georgia, 0.6/0.7in margins
- If it doesn't fit, cut the weakest bullets — don't shrink the font or margins
- 5-7 bullets from your most recent/relevant role (reordered by relevance), 1-2 per prior role
- Personal project bullets appear under a "Selected Projects" section only when filling a gap — they don't get their own section by default

## Tone Consistency

The resume and cover letter should feel like they were written by the same person. The cover letter is conversational narrative; the resume is tighter but still in your voice — especially the summary. A reader should move between them without a tonal whiplash.

For your full communication style (including how to answer application questions), see `communication-style.md`. The formal-deliverable register from that guide applies here: clean, grammatical prose that carries your instincts — compression, specificity, directness, zero filler — without the casualness of your Slack voice.
